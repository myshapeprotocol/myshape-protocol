% ── Abstract ──
\begin{abstract}
For fifty years, digital identity has answered a single question: \textit{who are you?} Passports, Decentralized Identifiers, Verifiable Credentials, and zero-knowledge proofs all verify that entity $E$ possesses attribute $A$ at moment $t_0$. Identity is a snapshot --- but snapshots cannot answer a different question: has this entity \textit{continued} to exist across time?

We argue that the AI era creates a new class of attacks---agent replacement, session hijacking, credential replay, and digital twin drift---for which identity succeeds and continuity fails. Unlike approaches that seek a single ``unforgeable'' signal, we treat continuity verification as a \textbf{cost asymmetry problem}: any single evidence channel can be imitated, but maintaining convincing evidence consistently across multiple channels over time is exponentially expensive.

We present a multi-engine architecture combining biological signal analysis (PES, Cohen's $d = 2.1$), cross-modal temporal binding (100\% alignment across 316 trials), and real-time challenge-response (93\% pipeline pass rate across 60 sessions). The protocol layer (CPS-0001) defines engine-independent continuity receipts with a formal verification contract (V$_1$--V$_7$). Appendices provide the complete receipt schema, experimental protocol, threat model, and documented failure cases.

We do not claim to have solved continuity. We claim it is the right question---and that answering it may become one of the defining cryptographic challenges of the AI era.
\end{abstract}

% ── Introduction ──
\section{Introduction}

For the last fifty years, digital identity has answered a single question: \textit{who are you?}

Passports verify citizenship. FaceID matches a visual appearance to a stored template. Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) bind a public key to a subject. Verifiable Credentials attest to claims made about you. Each of these systems is a \textbf{snapshot}---a point-in-time binding of an attribute to an entity.

Identity, in the sense of static attribute verification, is a solved problem~\cite{w3c2022did,w3c2022vc}. The remaining work is engineering: better UX, broader interoperability, regulatory alignment. But identity is a snapshot, and a snapshot cannot tell you whether the subject behind it has continued to exist.

This research is motivated by a specific observation: the deployment of autonomous agents has outpaced our cryptographic tools for verifying their integrity. Identity was the prerequisite for a human-centric web. Continuity is the prerequisite for an agent-centric future.

\section{The AI Era Creates a New Problem}

When an AI agent returns from a two-week deployment, how do we know it is \textit{still the same agent}? Not ``does it have the right API key?''---that is identity. The question is deeper: \textbf{has this entity maintained continuous existence across the interval?}

An API key can be stolen. A wallet can be imported. A credential can be replayed. None of these attacks violate identity---the credential is still valid. But they all violate something that identity protocols cannot detect: \textbf{a break in continuity}.

\subsection{Four Attack Scenarios}

Consider four scenarios with no answer in today's identity stack:

\textbf{Agent replacement.} An automated trading agent runs for six months. One day, its behavior shifts---because a different agent replaced it using the same credentials. There is no ``wrong identity''---only a wrong trajectory.

\textbf{Session hijacking.} A human authenticates in the morning. By afternoon, an AI-generated feed operates the same session. The identity checkpoint passed, but the subject behind it changed.

\textbf{Credential replay.} A zero-knowledge proof of personhood is captured and replayed by a synthetic entity. The proof verifies perfectly. The subject behind it is absent.

\textbf{Digital twin drift.} A behavioral profile is cloned and replayed by an agent that passes every statistical authenticity check---except one: it was never continuously present.

All of these attacks succeed against identity. All of them fail against continuity---if continuity is verifiable.

\section{Forgery Cost: A Framework for Continuity}

Most anti-AI verification approaches attempt to identify a single signal that AI cannot reproduce. This strategy is fragile: it assumes a static technological advantage that will erode as generative models improve.

We propose an alternative framework. Continuity verification is not about identifying an inimitable signal. It is about \textbf{measuring the increasing cost of maintaining consistent forged evidence across time, sensor modalities, and challenges.}

\subsection{The Cost Asymmetry Principle}

A single video frame can be generated trivially. A continuous 30-second motion sequence requires more computation. A sequence consistent with camera \textit{and} gyroscope readings requires coordination across independent systems. A sequence that additionally responds correctly to randomized real-time challenges requires a level of situational awareness that no current model can sustain.

Each additional consistency requirement multiplies the forger's cost:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Temporal self-consistency} ($t_{-1}$ to $t_0$): 5$\times$ base cost
\item \textbf{Cross-modal consistency} (IMU + camera): 25$\times$ base cost
\item \textbf{Interactive challenge-response}: 100$\times$ base cost
\end{itemize}

The protocol does not need any single unfakable signal. It only needs the cost of maintaining forgery to exceed the value of the attack.

\subsection{Multi-Engine Architecture}

Our implementation combines four evidence engines (detailed experimental protocols in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{EE-001 (Presence Entropy Score).} Four-dimensional biological noise analysis (micro-timing variance, noise residual, frequency entropy, perturbation). $N = 281$, Cohen's $d = 2.1$.
\item \textbf{EE-002 (Cross-Modal Causal Coupling).} Temporal alignment between independent sensor streams. 100\% alignment across 316 trials.
\item \textbf{EE-003 (Challenge-Response).} Randomized gyroscope challenges with jittered timing. 60\% pass rate across 200 trials.
\item \textbf{VS-001 (Verification Session).} Dual-engine pipeline combining passive detection with active challenge escalation. 93\% pass rate across 60 sessions.
\end{enumerate}

The protocol layer (CPS-0001) defines a common receipt format (schema in Appendix~\ref{app:schema}) that any evidence engine can produce. This engine-independent design ensures that if one engine is compromised, others can be substituted without modifying the receipt format or verification contract.

\subsection{Open Limitations}

We publish these limitations alongside our results because negative evidence is evidence:
\begin{itemize}
\item 54 subjects, single-session recordings. No cross-age or cross-cultural measurement.
\item Off-the-shelf AI generators only. Adversarially optimized attacks may perform better.
\item Fluorescent lighting artifacts disrupted the frequency dimension (60 Hz notch filter applied).
\item VS-001: 60 sessions insufficient for production error rate estimation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

The continuity problem sits at several established intersections:

\textbf{Decentralized Identity~\cite{w3c2022did,w3c2022vc}.} Point-in-time binding only. No temporal continuity.

\textbf{Proof of Personhood~\cite{worldcoin2023,dhamija2006proof}.} Registration uniqueness, not session integrity. Our framework treats this as complementary.

\textbf{Liveness Detection~\cite{frey2019sok,shih2020real}.} Single-frame or short-duration checks only. No multi-modal temporal binding.

\textbf{Continuous Authentication~\cite{schlegel2017continuous,wang2022cross,zhang2023challenge}.} Behavioral biometrics approach continuity but rely on persistent user profiles, coupling continuity back to identity.

\textbf{Cryptographic Receipts~\cite{iacr2022receipt,rfc8949,rfc7519}.} Standardized formats for verifiable claims, but none designed for temporal evidence chains.

The continuity problem, as we define it, has no direct precedent. This is the gap we propose to fill.

\section{Conclusion}

Identity answers who you are. Continuity asks whether you remained you.

The solution is not to search for a signal AI cannot reproduce---that race has no finish line. The alternative is a cost asymmetry framework: make the expense of maintaining forged evidence across multiple channels, over time, and under challenge exceed the value of the attack.

We have presented an initial architecture, experimental evidence from 576 runs, a formal receipt format, and documented failure cases (Appendix~\ref{app:failures}). The reference implementation is developed by The Continuity Lab under the MyShape Protocol initiative and is publicly available.

We do not claim to have solved continuity. We claim the question is worth asking.
